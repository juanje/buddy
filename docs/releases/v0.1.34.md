## Slack integration

Buddy can read your Slack. Set up session credentials in Settings →
Integrations and the assistant gains access to your threads, channel history,
and DMs — all through natural conversation.

**What you could see**

- **A new Slack panel in Settings.** The Integrations tab now has collapsible
  panels for each connector. Slack asks for your `xoxc` token and `xoxd`
  cookie, with a link to [slack-token-extractor](https://github.com/niclas3640/slack-token-extractor)
  for easy extraction.
- **Three Slack actions the assistant can use on your behalf.** Fetch a thread
  by URL, fetch channel or DM history by date range, and list your channels.
  Each fetch saves a local markdown file that the assistant reads — content
  stays on your machine.
- **User mentions are resolved to real names.** Raw IDs like `<@U0123ABCDE>`
  become `@Jane Smith` before you see the content. Resolved names are cached
  locally.
- **The assistant learns your contacts from use.** The first time you ask about
  a conversation with someone, it may need a URL. After that, it remembers the
  channel — including the other person's name for DMs — so next time it can
  fetch directly.
- **Enterprise Grid restrictions are handled gracefully.** Corporate workspaces
  that block channel listing get a clear message instead of repeated failed
  attempts. The assistant asks for a direct URL and moves on.
- **Results are cached and served offline.** Threads and channel history stay
  fresh for about an hour. Force-refresh is always available.

**What changed underneath**

Integration panels in Settings are now collapsible (collapsed by default,
status badge visible). The Slack connector reuses the same cache engine,
permission gate, and error handling as Jira, validating the infrastructure
is not Jira-shaped.
